He woke to the slant of afternoon light, amber and low, casting long shadows through the tangle of mangrove roots. His mouth was dry, his stomach clenched with hunger, but the sleep had restored something—a clarity, a sharpness that cut through the fog of exhaustion. He pushed himself up from the sand, brushed the grit from his palms, and began to walk the shoreline.

The beach curved inward, then gave way to a denser growth of brush and sawgrass. Here the sand was disturbed—not by the random wash of tide, but by something purposeful. He knelt, his fingers tracing the crushed stems of a clump of weeds, the soil churned and dark. A chase, he thought. Something had fled this way, and something else had followed.

His eyes caught a gleam in the low light. He picked it up: a brass cartridge, still faintly warm to the touch. He turned it over. .22 caliber. Small game, or perhaps something else. Nearby, the leaves were spattered with dried blood, brown and flaking. He touched a stain, brought his fingers to his nose. Fresh enough. Hours, no more.

He stood, scanning the ground. There—a print, sunk deep into the soft earth. The distinct heel and tread of a hunting boot. He followed it into the underbrush, pushing aside fronds that had been snapped at the stem. The trail was deliberate, unhurried. Whoever had made it knew the ground, knew the course.

He walked until the light began to fail, the shadows pooling under the trees. And then he saw it: a skeleton, lashed to a branch with vines. No, not a skeleton—a framework of bones, arranged with an artist's cruelty, the ribs curving like the bars of a cage. He stopped, his breath catching. A warning. Or a signature.

He pressed on, the trail leading him along the ridge of a bluff. The forest fell away, and he saw the sea below, dark and restless. And then he saw the lights.

They blazed from the windows of a château that rose from the cliff-top like a scar against the dusk—turrets and gables, leaded glass, the whole structure vast and improbable. He stood for a moment, the weariness flooding back into his limbs, but with it a wild, ragged hope. He descended the path to the gate: iron spikes, tall and black, flanked by stone pillars. He gripped the cold metal, shook it once, and felt it give.

A feral smile touched his lips. He was not alone.
