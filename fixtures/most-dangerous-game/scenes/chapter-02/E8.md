The bleeding from his shoulder had stopped, or at least slowed to a seeping that no longer marked his passage. That was something. Rainsford pushed deeper into the jungle as the light began to fail, the long shadows of afternoon merging into the amorphous gloom of dusk. His legs moved mechanically now, each step a separate decision, a negotiation with muscles that screamed for rest.

The ground changed without warning. One moment he was running on the familiar carpet of rotting leaves; the next, the earth gave way beneath him with a sucking sound, and he was knee-deep in something that pulled. Quicksand. The word flashed through his mind as he wrenched his leg free, the moccasin making a wet, obscene noise as it released. He stumbled backward, heart hammering, and stood at the edge of what he now recognized as Death Swamp.

The air here was different. Heavier. It carried the smell of decay and standing water, of things that had died slowly and been absorbed into the dark soil. The trees were hung with moss that swayed in no wind, and the ground pulsed with an unsettling softness underfoot.

Rainsford stood still, his breath coming in ragged gasps, and let the hunter in him take over. The soft earth. The sucking mud. The way the ground could be made to lie.

He began to dig.

His hands found the work before his mind fully acknowledged the plan. He scraped at the wet earth with a fallen branch, then with his bare hands, the soil packing under his nails as he widened and deepened a pit. The darkness closed in around him, and he worked by feel alone, his fingers seeking out saplings, stripping them of leaves, sharpening their ends against stone until they were rudimentary but lethal. He placed them in the pit, points upward, and covered the whole with a lattice of branches and weeds, and then with a carpet of moss that matched the surrounding ground.

When it was done, he crawled behind the blackened stump of a lightning-struck tree and pressed himself into the shadow, his knife slick in his palm.

He waited.

The swamp settled into its night rhythms around him — the croak of frogs, the distant splash of something heavy. Time passed in increments he could not measure. His eyes strained against the dark until shapes began to swim in the void.

Then he heard it. The soft tread of footsteps, too careful to be an animal. Zaroff was coming, and he was coming fast.

Rainsford watched the darkness resolve into a figure — tall, moving with that fluid grace that marked the hunter rather than the hunted. The silhouette reached the edge of the pit. The cover held.

Rainsford held his breath.

The figure stepped. The branches gave way with a terrible crack, and there was a sound — not a man's cry, but a yelp, high and sharp and animal, followed by the wet, splintering impact of something striking the stakes below.

Silence. Then a whimper, fading.

Zaroff's voice came out of the darkness, calm and amused. "I see you've killed one of my best hounds, Mr. Rainsford. A rather effective trap. You score again."

A pause. Rainsford could hear the smile in the next words. "But I shall return with the pack. All of them. And then we shall see."

The footsteps receded, and Rainsford was alone in the dark with the sound of his own breathing and the knowledge that the game had only just begun.
