The dining hall was a cathedral of slaughter. The heads of a hundred beasts lined the walls in concentric rows—elk, lion, Cape buffalo, tiger—their glass eyes gleaming in the candlelight like frozen accusations. Rainsford sat at the long oak table, exhaustion a leaden weight in his bones, yet his mind had never been more alive. Across the gulf of linen and silver, General Zaroff ate with the studied reverence of a man performing a sacrament.

Ivan served the borsch in silence, the earthy tang of beets and dill rising with the steam. Zaroff talked as he ate, his voice a smooth baritone that filled the hall. His Cossack youth. The blade and the horse. The fever of battle in the Tsar's army. Then the grand circumnavigation—shikar on every continent, the mastery of every beast that walked or crawled.

A shadow passed over the General's face as he poured the champagne.

"It ceased to amuse me," he said simply. "The plateau. The inevitable boredom of perfection." He leaned forward, his pale eyes luminous in the quivering light. "Can you understand that, Mr. Rainsford? A hunter of your caliber?"

"A hunter never loses the thrill," Rainsford heard himself say.

Zaroff smiled, slow and patient. "A hunter must have a worthy quarry. And I found that all animals, in the end, are the same. They lack the one element that makes the chase divine." He paused, savoring the silence. "Reason. Courage. The will to fight back. So I invented a new animal."

A terrible stillness crept into the room. Rainsford's hand, reaching for his glass, froze. The wine glowed like blood in the candlelight.

"What animal?" he whispered.

"Man," said Zaroff. "The most dangerous game."

The chair scraped against the stone as Rainsford rose. "You're a murderer."

"I am a huntsman," Zaroff corrected, his voice smooth as oil. "The weak exist for the amusement of the strong. It is the law of nature. You do not scruple over shooting a stag. Why should I scruple over a sailor who gives me a moment's true sport?"

"They are men. You run them down with hounds."
"My prey is given food, clothing, a head start. A fighting chance. It is fairer than war. If he eludes me for three days, he is freed."

"Have many won?"
Zaroff's smile deepened. "I have yet to lose one. You will hunt with me tomorrow, Sanger Rainsford."

"No. I will not."

Zaroff shrugged with perfect calm. "Ivan will take you to your room. We will speak again in the morning."

Ivan led him up a winding staircase to a room that was rich and cold—tapestries, a massive bed, a fire laid but unlit. The giant turned the key in the lock, and the heavy click echoed like a gunshot. Rainsford stood motionless in the center of the room, dressed in his host's fine clothes, listening to the footsteps fade down the corridor. He did not have to try the door. He already knew the shape of his cage.
