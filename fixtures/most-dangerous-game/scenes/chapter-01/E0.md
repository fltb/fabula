The sea was as black as ink, a great expanse of nothingness stretching out on all sides, and the yacht cut through it with a soft, rhythmic hiss. The air was warm and thick, heavy with salt and the faint, sickly-sweet smell of tropical decay. Rainsford leaned against the rail, his pipe cupped in his hand, the bowl a tiny orange coal in the darkness. Beside him, Whitney did the same, the glow of his own pipe illuminating his face in brief, flickering intervals.
