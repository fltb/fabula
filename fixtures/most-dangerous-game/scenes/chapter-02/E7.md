The footsteps were unhurried. A soft, rhythmic crunch of leaves. Through the tangle of ferns, Rainsford saw him. Zaroff walked with his rifle slung, his head tilted, reading the ground. He was a hound on a scent, beautiful and terrible in his focus. He paused, his polished boot hovering over the trigger bough.
        Rainsford's heart stopped.
        Zaroff’s head snapped up. A flicker of pure danger crossed his face. He didn't look at the tree; he felt it. He threw himself backward with a speed that defied his size.
        The dead tree groaned, fell. The air was displaced in a rush. The massive trunk caught Zaroff’s shoulder, a glancing, thunderous blow that spun him into the mud.
        Silence. A bird called out, startled.
        Then, a sound that cut through the jungle deeper than any gunshot. Laughter. A deep, rolling chuckle that grew into a full-bodied roar.
        Zaroff rose, brushing dirt from his impeccable coat. His shoulder was stained with blood.
        “Good God, Rainsford,” he called out, his voice carrying through the trees with awful cheerfulness. “A Malay man-catcher! You are full of surprises. You have managed to touch my shoulder. Bravo.” He chuckled, a warm, appreciative sound. “I will have to dress this. Such a clever fellow. Tomorrow, then.”
        He turned and walked away, his step light, his laugh echoing through the trees long after he was gone.
