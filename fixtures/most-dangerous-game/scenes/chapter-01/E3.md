The knocker was a coiled serpent cast in iron, its scales rough and cold against Rainsford’s palm. He lifted it and let it fall. The sound was not a ring but a deep, resonant thud that seemed to travel through the stone walls and into the earth beneath his feet. He waited, his breath shallow, his wet clothes clinging to him like a second skin.

The door swung inward without a creak, on hinges well-oiled and silent. And there stood a man who seemed carved from the same darkness as the château’s shadow. He was immense, a giant in a crimson sash, with a beard black as pitch and eyes that held no light. In his hand, a revolver—long-barreled, the muzzle a dark circle aimed directly at Rainsford’s heart.

Rainsford did not step back. He had faced charging boar and wounded leopard; he knew the stillness of the moment before violence. “I fell off a yacht,” he said, his voice steadier than he expected. “I need shelter.”

The giant did not answer. His thumb pulled back the hammer of the revolver with a crisp, metallic click that echoed in the cavernous hall beyond.

Before Rainsford could speak again, a voice came from the interior, smooth and cultured, like a blade drawn from silk. “Ivan, if you please.”

The giant’s eyes flicked to the side. From the shadows of the hall, a figure emerged—a tall, slender man in evening clothes, immaculate as if dressed for a dinner party rather than a night on a remote island. His face was aristocratic, with a high forehead and a thin mustache, and his smile was that of a host welcoming a long-expected guest.

“Mr. Rainsford, I presume,” the man said. “I recognized you from your work. The snow leopards of Tibet—a remarkable account.”

Rainsford’s jaw tightened. “You know me.”

“But of course. I am General Zaroff.” He gestured with a slight, elegant motion of his fingers. Ivan lowered the revolver and stepped back, disappearing into the gloom as silently as he had appeared. “Forgive my servant. He is deaf and mute—a condition that makes him an excellent watchdog, but a poor conversationalist. Please, come in. You look as though you have had a trying night.”

The door stood open. The warmth of the château spilled out, carrying the scent of woodsmoke and wine. Rainsford crossed the threshold, his bare feet silent on the stone, and the great door swung shut behind him.
